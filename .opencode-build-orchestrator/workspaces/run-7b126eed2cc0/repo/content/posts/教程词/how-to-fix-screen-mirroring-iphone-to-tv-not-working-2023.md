## Background

On iPhone, screen mirroring typically relies on AirPlay. Apple’s support guidance is clear on the essentials: your iPhone and the receiving device need to be on the same Wi-Fi network, you start the process from Control Center, and you may need to enter an AirPlay passcode shown on the TV. Apple also notes that some video apps do not support AirPlay in the same way, which explains why one app may work while another does not.

That matters because many people treat every mirroring failure as the same issue. In practice, there are at least three different situations. First, the TV may not support AirPlay at all. Second, the TV may support AirPlay, but the two devices are not discovering each other because of network or settings problems. Third, the iPhone may connect correctly, but the app or content itself refuses to play through AirPlay.

If you keep those three buckets in mind, the troubleshooting process becomes much simpler. You are not trying to fix everything at once. You are narrowing the problem down.

## What To Pay Attention To

Start with compatibility. If you are trying to mirror directly to a smart TV, it needs to support AirPlay. Apple’s instructions refer specifically to Apple TV, AirPlay-compatible smart TVs, and certain Macs. If your TV is older or uses a different wireless casting standard, your iPhone may never show it as a valid screen mirroring target. In that case, the problem is not a broken iPhone feature. It is a mismatch between devices.

Next, check the network carefully. Apple says both devices should be on the same Wi-Fi network. That sounds obvious, but it is one of the most common failure points. A dual-band router can make this easy to miss if one device is on one network name and the other is on another. If the iPhone and TV are not on the same local network, discovery may fail completely.

Then use the correct mirroring path on the iPhone. Open Control Center, tap **Screen Mirroring**, and look for the TV in the list. If the TV appears, select it. If a passcode shows up on the TV, enter that passcode on the iPhone. This step matters because users sometimes confuse app-level AirPlay streaming with full screen mirroring. If your goal is to duplicate the entire iPhone display, use the Screen Mirroring control rather than just the video player’s AirPlay icon.

If the TV does not appear at all, restart both devices before moving on. This is basic advice, but it is still one of the most reliable troubleshooting steps because device discovery can hang in ways that are not visible from the interface. Restarting the router can help too if both devices seem fine individually but still refuse to see each other.

Software version is another point worth checking. Apple’s support pages repeatedly assume current software. If your iPhone or TV software is outdated, AirPlay behavior can become inconsistent. Updating both sides is not a magic fix, but it is a reasonable step if the connection used to work and suddenly stopped.

Finally, pay attention to the app you are using. Apple notes that some video apps may not support AirPlay. That means your iPhone might mirror normally from the Home Screen or Photos app, yet a specific streaming app may block playback, black out video, or refuse to hand off the stream. If that happens, test mirroring with something simple first, such as Photos or a non-protected video clip. That tells you whether the problem is the mirroring setup itself or the app’s own restrictions.

## FAQ

## Why is my TV not showing up in Screen Mirroring on iPhone?

The most likely reasons are lack of AirPlay support, different Wi-Fi networks, or a temporary discovery issue. Check whether the TV is an AirPlay-compatible smart TV or connected through Apple TV. Then verify both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network and restart them if needed.

## Why does my iPhone connect but nothing appears on the TV?

If the connection starts but the screen does not display correctly, the first things to check are the AirPlay passcode prompt, network stability, and whether the TV is actually set to receive AirPlay. It is also possible that the specific app you are using does not support AirPlay playback properly, even if the mirroring link itself is active.

## Do I need Apple TV to mirror my iPhone to a TV?

Not always. Apple says you can mirror to Apple TV, an AirPlay-compatible smart TV, or certain Macs. If your television has built-in AirPlay support, you do not need a separate Apple TV box. If it does not, Apple TV is one common way to add that compatibility.

## Is screen mirroring the same as streaming with AirPlay?

No. Streaming with AirPlay can send only the video or audio from a supported app, while screen mirroring duplicates what is on the iPhone screen. If you are troubleshooting, this distinction matters because one method may work even when the other does not.

## If I searched for how to fix screen mirroring iphone to tv not working 2023, what should I try first?

Try the shortest, highest-value path first: confirm AirPlay compatibility, make sure both devices are on the same Wi-Fi network, open Control Center and tap Screen Mirroring, enter the passcode if prompted, and restart both devices if the TV still does not appear. That sequence resolves a large share of real-world failures.
