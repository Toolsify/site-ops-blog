## Background

The phrase “solve a Rubik’s Cube in one minute” can mean two very different things. For a complete beginner, it can sound like learning the whole puzzle from scratch and somehow hitting a one-minute solve almost immediately. In practice, that is not how most people get there. On Reddit, people asking about sub-1 often already know a basic layer-by-layer method and want to cut down pauses, fix inefficient steps, or decide whether to learn CFOP.

That distinction matters. If you do not yet know how to solve a 3x3 at all, the real first goal is simply finishing consistently. If you already solve in 90 seconds, 2 minutes, or a little more, then the sub-1 target becomes much more actionable. In several Reddit discussions, cubers describe reaching or returning to one-minute times with either a polished beginner method or an early CFOP approach using intuitive F2L and simple last-layer subsets such as 2-look OLL and 2-look PLL. Others point out that a poor cube can slow progress, especially if pieces catch, corner-twist, or resist quick turns.

So when people ask how to solve a rubik s cube 3x3 in 1 minute, the honest answer is this: you are usually not chasing a brand-new solve method. You are learning to solve the same puzzle with fewer stops, fewer wasted moves, and better step-to-step flow.

## Why this matters

A one-minute solve is a useful benchmark because it forces you to think about solving quality, not just cube theory. Below that mark, obvious inefficiencies start to matter. A cross that takes too long, awkward regrips, hunting for pieces after every step, or repeatedly fixing the same mistaken pair will cost more time than most learners expect.

Reddit advice is helpful here because it is less abstract than many “ultimate speedcubing guides.” Instead of pretending there is one magic trick, posters repeatedly recommend a handful of practical improvements:

- Use a decent speed cube instead of a sticky entry-level cube.
- Learn finger tricks so turns flow instead of resetting your grip constantly.
- Practice planning earlier, especially during the cross.
- Reduce pauses by looking for the next piece before you finish the current one.
- Move past a purely basic beginner method if your progress has stalled.

That is why the one-minute target matters. It gives your practice direction. You stop asking, “What algorithm should I learn next?” and start asking, “Where am I really losing time?” For most solvers, that question produces better results than chasing advanced concepts too early.

## What to pay attention to

If your goal is specifically **how to solve a rubik s cube 3x3 in 1 minute**, focus on the parts of the solve that Reddit users keep identifying as the real bottlenecks.

First, pay attention to your cross. Even in beginner-friendly discussions, cubers emphasize that cross efficiency matters a lot. If you spend too long finding edge pieces or rotate the cube repeatedly before starting, your solve is already behind pace. You do not need elite-level cross planning, but you do need a repeatable habit: inspect, choose a side, and solve the cross with intention instead of improvising every turn.

Second, watch your transition out of the cross. This is where many solves fall apart. A lot of Reddit replies to sub-1 questions mention F2L, beginner F2L, or at least pairing corners and edges more intelligently than a strict corner-first, edge-second beginner method. Even if full CFOP feels too heavy at first, learning how to insert pairs more naturally can cut a large chunk of time. The important part is not speed alone. It is recognition. You want to know what you are about to do before your hands stop moving.

Third, treat pauses as the main enemy. Many people think they need faster fingers, but Reddit advice often points in a different direction: slow down to learn smoother solving. That sounds backward, yet it makes sense. If you turn quickly and then freeze to search, your overall time stays high. If you turn a little slower but keep moving, your average often improves. Smooth solves win more than frantic solves.

Fourth, your hardware matters, but only to a point. In multiple Reddit threads, posters say that almost any proper speed cube feels better than a standard Rubik’s brand cube. That does not mean a new cube will magically make you sub-1. It means bad hardware can make good habits harder to build. If your current cube catches often or feels inconsistent, replacing it can remove a genuine obstacle.

Finally, do not overcomplicate last layer too early. For a sub-1 target, you usually do not need an advanced algorithm set. Reddit replies often steer learners toward simple last-layer systems first, especially 2-look approaches. That keeps your mental load manageable while you improve the bigger time sinks: recognition, turning flow, and piece tracking.

## FAQ

### Can a beginner really learn how to solve a rubik s cube 3x3 in 1 minute?

Yes, but “beginner” needs context. If you mean someone who just learned the basic solve yesterday, one minute is ambitious. If you mean someone who already solves consistently and is trying to improve, Reddit discussions suggest sub-1 is very reachable with better efficiency, smoother turning, and either a refined beginner method or beginner CFOP.

### Do I need CFOP to get under one minute?

Not strictly. Reddit threads include people saying the beginner method can reach one minute or even faster with enough practice. But many cubers still recommend moving toward CFOP or at least beginner F2L because it scales better and helps cut unnecessary moves.

### Is a better cube necessary?

Not always, but it can matter. Reddit users frequently mention that poor cubes create lockups and awkward turns that interrupt learning. A decent speed cube does not replace practice, yet it makes it easier to practice correctly.

### What should I practice first if I am stuck around 1 minute 30 seconds?

Start with cross efficiency, transitions into first pairs, and reducing pauses. Those three areas come up constantly in Reddit advice. Learning a few finger tricks and using slow, deliberate solves can help more than rushing into lots of new algorithms.

### Should I solve faster or slower in practice?

Usually slower at first. Many cubers on Reddit recommend controlled solves so you can see the next piece before finishing the current step. That builds lookahead and reduces dead time, which is one of the biggest differences between a 90-second solve and a 55-second solve.
